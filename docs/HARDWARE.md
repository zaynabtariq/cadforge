# Hardware assumptions and V2 packaging

V2 is a source-grounded geometric prototype, not production-ready glasses. The Pi's 65 mm direction follows the right temple, and the camera sits above the right lens facing forwards. The rigid frame and two pod bases form one connected chassis. Separate lids expose the electronics. Mounting posts follow the nominal PCB hole patterns; holes continue through the lids and pod floor for a through-bolt retention concept. Hardware length, nuts, PCB compression and tool access still need a physical prototype. These are not modeled heat-set inserts or certified threaded joints.

## Primary sources checked

- [Official Zero 2 W mechanical drawing](https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/rpizero2/raspberry-pi-zero-2-w-mechanical-drawing.pdf): 65 × 30 mm outline; mounting axes 3.5 mm from corresponding edges, therefore 58 × 23 mm pitch. The source gives connector center stations 12.4, 41.4 and 54 mm. The drawing does not dimension complete plug insertion/overmould envelopes or the tallest populated-board component.
- [Official original Zero drawing](https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/rpizero/raspberry-pi-zero-mechanical-drawing.pdf): specifies M2.5 mounting holes drilled to 2.75 ± 0.05 mm. V2 uses a nominal 2.75 mm Pi bore. This is cross-family corroboration, not a tolerance certification for a purchased Zero 2 W.
- [Official Camera Module 3 Standard drawing](https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/camera/camera-module-3-standard-mechanical-drawing.pdf): 25 × 23.862 mm board outline, 1.12 mm PCB thickness, 11.3 mm overall depth, 2.2 mm holes. With origin at lower left in its front view, hole axes are (2,2), (23,2), (2,14.5), (23,14.5) mm. Lens axis is (12.5,14.4) mm; its position differs from the upper mounting row by 0.1 mm. V2 conservatively reserves the complete 11.3 mm depth above its support plane instead of guessing a detailed populated surface.
- [Official camera documentation](https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/accessories/camera.html): Module 3 retains Module 2's mounting pattern, but changes to sensor position and size mean it does not fit the Zero case camera lid. No Module 2 case assumptions were imported.
- [Official computer documentation](https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/computers/raspberry-pi.html): Zero uses a mini 22-pin 0.5 mm pitch CSI connector; the Pi end needs a compatible cable rather than assuming the regular 15-pin cable fits.

The connector identities follow the official Zero 2 W layout. V2 opens approximate mini-HDMI, USB data, USB power, microSD and CSI access spaces. Their plug widths/heights and insertion depths are explicit engineering assumptions, not sourced manufacturer plug drawings. GPIO/header access remains unresolved. Pi populated height is 6 mm and PCB thickness 1.6 mm as provisional model inputs. Purchase and measure the actual board, camera, adapter cable and plugs before releasing drawings.

## Editable contract

Use `production_geometry.layout(spec)` for nominal component sizes, mounting axes, local-to-world transforms, source URLs and assumptions. `transform(shape, record)` maps local probes to the assembly. The Pi uses a 90-degree Y rotation: its long axis runs towards the front along negative Z, placing its CSI connector towards the camera. Camera Y rotation is 180 degrees, directing its lens along negative Z. X is wearer left/right, Y is vertical and +Z runs rearward along the temples.

`bore_diameter` and `boss_outer_diameter` are editable numeric parameters, enabling a learned fastener-clearance command to alter actual BRep holes and support posts. Default Pi values are 2.75 and 6.75 mm. `camera_bore_diameter` defaults to 2.2 mm; camera boss diameter defaults to the drawing's 4.75 mm mounting land. `lens_opening_diameter` defaults to a provisional 12 mm opening. Changing a hole or boss requires validation against the real PCB; learned commands do not override physical mounting-hole limits.

Build with `production_geometry.build_design(DesignSpec(family='glasses'))`, and use its `export_design` to preserve the correct editable regeneration backend. Export includes `hardware_layout.json` alongside STEP/STL/SVG/Python/JSON. Printed parts are `chassis`, `pi_lid`, `camera_lid` and `cable_guide_*` (separate routing segments). Board/reference envelopes are probes, not printable solids.

## Verification scope and remaining work

Tests measure actual BRep solid validity, non-intersection, component and connector free spaces, mounting apertures, connected cable routing space and editable export regeneration. External engineering gates should independently reconstruct source-grounded probes from the layout contract. Artifact probes alone are not an adversarial validity oracle.

The guide connects the two nominal cable ports geometrically. Its rectangular corners do not establish a manufacturable FPC bend radius, conductor strain, folding compatibility, slack or fatigue life. Select a 22-to-15-pin compatible camera cable, obtain its dimensional drawing and bend specification, replace the corners with a validated route, and test it on hardware. The guide requires its own assembly/retention decision; it is not a demonstrated strain-relief clamp.

Vent apertures are geometric openings only. There is no thermal analysis, battery/power subsystem, moisture/dust rating, skin-contact certification, optical field-of-view proof, lens protection, fatigue result, mass-balance assessment or wearability evidence. The integrated rigid chassis requires print-orientation/support and postprocessing review and does not fold. A numerical geometry pass must not be reported as production readiness.

An independent full-bore gate found 0.088588 mm³ of guide material intruding into a camera mounting hole after narrower diagnostic probes had passed. The generator was corrected to trim guide pieces with the complete nominal bore plus numerical tolerance, retaining the stricter gate. This is a recorded development self-correction, not a changed acceptance threshold.

A second development transfer failure appeared at the sourced 52 × 42.4 mm lens frame and 145 mm temple: the pod's fixed world Y offset no longer reached the taller temple, producing a disconnected chassis. V2 now solves a reusable mating-anchor translation: the pod's upper Y face aligns to `lens_height + wall`, and its rear Z anchor is `0.6 × temple_length`. This preserves the requested frame dimensions. Cable routing endpoints and all intermediate offsets derive from the transformed CSI and camera connector envelopes. A 27-case development sweep spans lens widths 48/52/54 mm, heights 34/42.4/46 mm and temples 140/145/150 mm; it checks connected chassis material, protected openings and cable-port connection. This expands tested geometric coverage without claiming fit to a person's head or a validated FPC bend.

## Machine-readable camera interconnect

`camera_interconnect.py` now records the required Standard–Mini family in every new production `hardware_layout.json`. The standard cable supplied with the camera fails the Zero connector-family check. This follows the [official installation documentation](https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/accessories/camera.html), checked 2026-09-12. The [official Camera Cable product](https://www.raspberrypi.com/products/camera-cable/) lists 200/300/500 mm lengths. No length is selected until the route, slack and connector terminations are specified.

The checker separates connector-family matching, externally supplied length sufficiency, and physical qualification. Matching endpoint families does not authenticate arbitrary third-party wiring or contact orientation. The physical gate stays blocked even when family and length checks pass. Width/thickness, minimum bend radius, twists, latch access, retention and powered signal integrity remain unresolved.

`artifacts/production/interconnect-audit/` contains a fresh editable reference export and `interconnect-assessment.json`. It preserves the prior geometry design while attaching the verified interconnect requirement; no previous artifact or benchmark is rewritten. Eleven tests cover the incompatible supplied cable, endpoint order, mismatched families, insufficient/invalid lengths and blocked production status. This is a deterministic assembly prerequisite, not evidence of a physically assembled camera. No model calls were used for the rebuild; authoring costs are not fully metered.
