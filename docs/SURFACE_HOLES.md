# Reusing blind drilling on selected faces

The editor now sends the geometric outward normal of the face hit by the actual mouse click. For non-Z faces, a finite-depth hole request becomes `drill_surface_hole`. The operation constructs a right-handed rigid frame with that normal as local Z, invokes the existing corrected `drill_blind_hole`, and transforms the result back. It inherits the depth, disk-floor topology, stock coverage, area/volume and closed-mesh checks. No separate weaker side-hole cutter was substituted.

Supported entries are planar exterior support faces: the entire part lies behind the selected face plane. Recessed faces, curved entries and arbitrary angled drilling are not supported by this composition. A side-face through-hole request currently asks for a finite depth instead of silently executing a Z cut. Explicit axis or angle instructions also require clarification rather than disappearing.

## Failures and corrections

All six arbitrarily rotated STL positives initially failed the inherited stock-coverage check. Binary STL rounding leaves tiny differences between a nominal face plane and serialized vertices. The adapter now performs an explicit bounded local support-plane normalization while retaining all connectivity and openings. It also normalizes the local mesh to the Boolean kernel's float32 representation before validation: the public angle-block exposed a false removed-volume discrepancy when the kernel performed this conversion implicitly.

No inherited blind-hole thresholds were relaxed. The repair budget is `max(1e-6 mm, local thickness × 1e-7)`. Source geometry hashes, affected vertex counts, constituent displacement bounds and the combined `precision_normalization_mm` bound are recorded in feature metadata and checks. The preview discloses the correction before Apply. After the Boolean operation, surviving vertices with a unique source match are restored to their original coordinates. A separate gate compares final removed volume against the original imported stock at the existing relative tolerance of 5e-6.

The inherited floor and coverage checks operate on the explicitly normalized local geometry. Final world checks cover original-stock removed volume, winding, watertightness and finite coordinates; they do not independently rerun all local checks after source-vertex restoration. This distinction and the Boolean-kernel resolution bound remain limitations, not an exact manufacturing guarantee.

## Independent evidence

`tests/test_surface_hole.py` freezes 21 development cases: all six faces before and after a 37-degree rotation about [1,2,3] plus translation; invalid normals/entries/depth; and the rotated tiny-through-hole counterexample. Exported meshes independently retain every original corner exactly, achieve the requested depth/removed volume, remain watertight and undo exactly. The tiny-void rejection remains active. Planner tests separately check measured normals, units, adversarial model output, explicit orientation conflicts, and missing-depth side holes.

The first browser attempt used the plate fixture's rounded edge. Its actual measured normal contradicted the test's assumption of a flat X face. That failed selection is retained under `artifacts/surface-hole-studio-test/first-selection-failure.json`; it made no model call. The final browser test uses the public `angle_block.STL` and explicitly selects inches at import. STL carries no unit metadata; this is a declared demonstration unit, not an inferred manufacturing specification. A real side-view click and natural-language request exercise the full planner/API/geometry/Apply/Export/Undo path. The exported result is independently measured in world coordinates.
